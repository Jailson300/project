the unique cuisines of Meitei

Rice, known as Chak in Meitei, is the heart of Meitei cuisine and culture. It is more than just a staple food—it is deeply connected to daily life, agriculture, festivals, and religious ceremonies. A traditional Meitei meal is incomplete without rice, which is typically served alongside fish, seasonal vegetables, herbs, and fermented ingredients like ngari (fermented fish)

Fish is one of the most important ingredients in Meitei cuisine, serving as the primary source of protein for many households. Blessed with numerous rivers, lakes, and wetlands—especially the famous Loktak Lake—the Meitei people have long relied on freshwater fish as a dietary staple. Fish is enjoyed in fresh, dried, smoked, and fermented forms, making it an essential part of daily meals as well as festive occasions.

Popular dishes

KANGSHOI

Kangshoi, also known as Chamthong, is a traditional Meitei vegetable stew and one of the healthiest dishes in Manipuri cuisine. It is a light, mildly flavored broth prepared by boiling seasonal vegetables with local herbs and a small amount of salt. Fresh fish or ngari (fermented fish) may be added to enhance its flavor.

NGA-THONGBA

Nga Thongba is a traditional Meitei fish curry and one of the most beloved dishes in Manipuri cuisine. The word "Nga" means fish, and "Thongba" means curry. Prepared primarily with freshwater fish, seasonal vegetables, and aromatic local herbs, Nga Thongba is a staple dish in Meitei households and is usually eaten with steamed rice (chak).

OOTI

Ooti is a traditional Meitei delicacy made primarily from green peas (hawai-mana) and is one of the most distinctive dishes in Manipuri cuisine. Known for its creamy texture and mild flavor, Ooti is usually prepared by boiling peas with a small amount of alkaline soda (khar), which gives the dish its characteristic softness and light green appearance. It is commonly served with steamed rice (chak) and other traditional dishes.

EROMBA

Eromba is one of the most iconic and beloved dishes of Meitei cuisine. It is a spicy mashed preparation made by combining boiled vegetables, fermented fish (ngari), and chillies into a flavourful mixture. Known for its distinctive aroma and fiery taste, Eromba is considered a symbol of Manipuri culinary identity and is an essential part of traditional Meitei meals.

Popular snack's

BORA

Bora is a traditional Meitei fried snack made primarily from ground pulses, especially black gram (urad dal) or Bengal gram. Crispy on the outside and soft on the inside, Bora is a popular accompaniment to tea and is commonly prepared during family gatherings, festivals, and community events.

SINGJU

Singju is a traditional Meitei spicy salad made from a mixture of fresh vegetables, herbs, and aromatic seasonings. Renowned for its crunchy texture and bold flavours, Singju is one of the most iconic dishes of Manipuri cuisine and is enjoyed both as a snack and as a side dish with meals.

KELLI CHANA

Kelli Chana is a popular traditional snack in Meitei cuisine made from roasted chickpeas (chana). Simple yet nutritious, it is commonly enjoyed as an evening snack and is often sold by street vendors and local markets across Manipur.

Pakora Thongba

Pakora Thongba is a traditional Meitei curry prepared with pakoras (fried gram flour fritters) cooked in a lightly spiced gravy. The word "Thongba" means curry, and the dish is commonly served with steamed rice (chak). It is especially popular as a comforting homemade meal and during family gatherings.It is also popularly eaten as a snack with Cha-ngang(Red Tea).

UNIQUE INGREDIENTS

Chak Hao (Black rice)

Chak-Hao is the famous black aromatic rice of Manipur and one of the state's most treasured agricultural products. When cooked, the grains turn a deep purple colour and release a distinctive natural fragrance. Chak-Hao is widely used in both traditional meals and desserts, most notably Chak-Hao Kheer.

YONGCHAK

Yongchak, scientifically known as Parkia timoriana, is a traditional and highly valued ingredient in Meitei cuisine. It is the edible pod of the tree bean and is known for its distinctive aroma and crunchy texture. Yongchak is widely used in dishes such as Eromba, Singju, and various chutneys, adding a unique flavour that is characteristic of Manipuri food.

NGARI

Ngari is a traditional fermented freshwater fish and is considered the cornerstone of Meitei cuisine. It is prepared by fermenting fish for several months, resulting in a rich, umami flavour that enhances many traditional dishes. Rather than being eaten alone, Ngari is primarily used as a seasoning.

Popular dessert

CHAK HAO KHEER

Chak-Hao Kheer is a traditional Meitei dessert prepared from Chak-Hao, the aromatic black rice of Manipur. Renowned for its natural fragrance and deep purple colour, the dessert is made by slowly cooking black rice with milk and sugar until it reaches a rich, creamy consistency. It is one of the most celebrated sweet dishes of Manipuri cuisine and is often served during festivals, weddings, and special occasions.

SANGOM KHEER

Sangom Kheer is a traditional Meitei sweet dish prepared by cooking sangom (vermicelli) with milk and sugar until it becomes rich and creamy. Delicately flavoured and easy to prepare, it is a popular dessert in Meitei households and is often served during festivals, family celebrations, and special occasions.

MODUJAN

Modujan is a traditional Meitei sweet delicacy prepared from rice flour, sugar or jaggery, and coconut, often shaped into small balls or cakes. Soft, mildly sweet, and aromatic, Modujan is commonly prepared during festivals, religious ceremonies, and family celebrations in Manipur.

CREAMY FRUIT SALAD

Fruit Salad is a creamy dessert prepared by combining fresh seasonal fruits with chilled custard made from milk and custard powder. It is a refreshing and colourful dish that is commonly served during celebrations, family gatherings, and festive occasions.It has become a part of modern meitei feasts such as Weddings, Heijinpot's, etc
