HISTORICAL SITES

KANGLA

The ancient heart of Manipur

Once the royal seat of the Meitei rulers, Kangla carries centuries of history, tradition, and spiritual significance. Its ancient ruins, sacred sites, and majestic surroundings tell stories of Manipur's glorious past. Today, Kangla stands as a powerful symbol of Meitei heritage and identity. A place where the history of Manipur comes alive.

PAKHANGBA LAISHANG

A sacred connection to Meitei tradition

Dedicated to Pakhangba, a revered deity in ancient Meitei belief, the sacred site holds deep spiritual and cultural importance. Pakhangba is traditionally associated with the divine serpent-dragon form and the protection of the land and people. The Laishang reflects the enduring relationship between spirituality, nature, mythology, and Meitei identity. A sacred space where ancient beliefs continue to live.

KHONGJOM-WAR-MEMORIAL

A land where courage became legend.

The Khongjom War Memorial commemorates the brave warriors who fought against the British in the Anglo-Manipur War of 1891. The historic site remembers their extraordinary courage, determination, and ultimate sacrifice. Its peaceful surroundings stand in contrast to the fierce battle once fought here. A timeless tribute to the spirit of Manipur's heroes.

IMA MARKET

The market where women lead and tradition thrives

Located in the heart of Imphal, this historic market is uniquely run and managed by women, traditionally known as Imas or mothers. Its vibrant stalls showcase local vegetables, fish, handwoven textiles, traditional crafts, and everyday treasures. More than a marketplace, Ima Keithel represents the economic strength, independence, and cultural identity of Manipuri women. A living marketplace with a soul of its own.

SHAHEED-MINAR

A monument that remembers the fallen.

Standing proudly in Imphal, the monument honours the brave soldiers who sacrificed their lives during the Anglo-Manipur War of 1891. Its towering structure represents courage, resistance, and the enduring spirit of the people of Manipur. It serves as a reminder that the stories of those who fought for their land should never be forgotten. A symbol of sacrifice, pride, and remembrance.
